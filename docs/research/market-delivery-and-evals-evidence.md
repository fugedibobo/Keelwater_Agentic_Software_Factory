# Research: claims-software market, AI delivery evidence, PM time, LLM-as-judge practice

Researched 02/11/2026. FOUND = checked at the source URL; INFERRED = reasoned.

## Q1. Claims software market, release adoption, regulation

1. FOUND vendors (Straits, https://straitsresearch.com/report/claims-management-market): Guidewire, CCC, Sapiens, Duck Creek, Mitchell, Solera, Shift, Snapsheet, Origami, Ventiv, BriteCore, Verisk, Earnix. DataIntelo (https://dataintelo.com/report/global-claims-management-software-market) adds FINEOS, Insurity, Oracle, SAP, Pega, Salesforce.
2. FOUND size estimates, all from market-research vendors: Straits $35.14B in 2025 (7.49% CAGR to $67.32B in 2034); DataIntelo $4.8B in 2025 ($11.2B in 2034, 9.8%); Insight Partners (https://www.theinsightpartners.com/reports/insurance-claims-management-software-market) $549.48M in 2025 ($833.43M in 2034).
3. INFERRED: the 64x spread comes from scope definitions, so none of these sizes is usable. Use named-company revenue as the anchor.
4. FOUND Guidewire, year to 31 Jul 2026 (SEC 8-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001528396/000152839626000039/gwreex991earningsrelease73.htm): revenue $1,475.4M (+23%), ARR $1,242M, "More than 570 insurers in 44 countries".
5. FOUND Guidewire update automation (https://www.guidewire.com/resources/blog/technology/make-painful-upgrades-a-thing-of-the-past-with-update-automation-for.md): upgrades in "weeks instead of months (or even years)", one customer in under a week. The customer layer (UI, custom code, integrations) "still needs customer testing".
6. FOUND Duck Creek Active Delivery (https://www.duckcreek.com/resource/blog/understanding-the-benefits-of-duck-creek-active-delivery/): updates "every two weeks"; impactful updates are feature-flagged; "customers decide when" they take effect; effort "not completely eliminated, but you can plan for that effort".
7. FOUND Atlassian Cloud release tracks (https://jira.atlassian.com/browse/CLOUD-10919): customer suggestion opened 26 Sep 2019, resolved 3 Feb 2021, 7 votes and 19 watchers, goal "control the rate of change". Tracks are Continuous, Bundled and Preview (https://www.atlassian.com/software/premium/manage-change-in-cloud). The page does not say customers asked for them.
8. FOUND other cadences: Salesforce has 3 releases a year with a 6-week sandbox preview (https://www.salesforceben.com/why-admins-should-beware-of-salesforce-sandbox-preview-orgs/, secondary). The Coforge blog (https://blog.coforge.com/blog/continuous-saas-release-readiness-for-duck-creek-guidewire-upgrades) cites "lengthy regression cycles" and freeze periods, and suggests 1-2 weeks to absorb minor releases and a quarter for feature releases. It gives no figures.
9. NOT FOUND: any survey of insurers asking vendors for slower releases. INFERRED: demand shows up as customer-side controls (flags, tracks, previews, notice), not as slower deploys.
10. FOUND UK: PRA SS1/21 covers "UK Solvency II firms, the Society of Lloyd's, and its managing agents" (https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2021/march/operational-resilience-impact-tolerances-for-important-business-services-ss). FCA PS21/3 and SS1/21 required compliance by 31 Mar 2025, and firms must stay within impact tolerance even when a third party delivers the service (https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/01/07/uk-operational-resilience-rules-are-you-ready-for-31-march-2025/).
11. FOUND PRA SS2/21, March 2026 update, effective 18 Mar 2027, issued with PS7/26 (https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/supervisory-statement/2026/ss221-march-2026-update.pdf): para 6.4 expects contracts to require notice of any development with "material or adverse impact"; para 5.19 requires PRA notification before "significantly changing" a material arrangement.
12. FOUND EU: DORA applies from 17 Jan 2025 and covers insurance and reinsurance undertakings (https://cssf.lu/en/digital-operational-resilience-act-dora). Art 9(4)(e) requires ICT changes to be "recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled manner" (https://www.digital-operational-resilience-act.com/Article_9.html). Art 30 requires SLA "updates and revisions", notice periods and reporting of material developments (https://www.digital-operational-resilience-act.com/Article_30.html).
13. INFERRED: I found no rule setting a minimum release notice period. The customer's controlled-change obligation is the lever, so announced releases and flags help insurers evidence control.
14. NOT FOUND: data on how insurers retrain claims handlers on product change.

## Q2. AI and delivery speed

15. FOUND DORA 2025 (https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report, 23 Sep 2025): nearly 5,000 respondents; 90% use AI; more than 80% believe productivity rose; 30% have little or no trust in AI code. AI now has a positive relationship with throughput and product performance, but "a negative relationship with software delivery stability". "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there."
16. FOUND the seven DORA capabilities (Faros summary, https://www.faros.ai/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-dora-report-2025): AI stance, data ecosystems, AI-accessible data, version control, small batches, user-centric focus, internal platforms. I read no effect sizes from the full report.
17. FOUND METR 2025 (https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/): 16 developers, 246 issues, tasks took 19% longer with AI. They predicted a 24% speedup and afterwards believed 20%. The CI was +2% to +39% (METR 2026 post).
18. FOUND METR update, 24 Feb 2026 (https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/): 10 returning developers -18% (CI -38% to +9%), 47 new developers -4% (CI -15% to +9%). Negative figures mean a speedup. METR calls this an "unreliable signal": 30-50% of developers skipped tasks they would not do without AI, and pay fell from $150 to $50 an hour. It says developers are "likely" more sped up now, with "only very weak evidence" on size.
19. FOUND other trials: Google, 96 engineers, about 21% faster (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944, summer 2024). Anthropic, 52 mostly junior engineers, about 2 minutes faster but not significant (p=0.391), quiz scores about 17% lower (https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills, 29 Jan 2026).
20. FOUND Harvard/Jellyfish paper (https://fion.ac/jellyfish.pdf): 445M work events, 655 firms, to Aug 2026. Agents: PRs +35.7%, commits +36.0%, issues resolved +13.7% (borderline); assistants +2.0% issues, not significant. Submission-to-approval +56.1%, backlog wait +59.9%, active review +58.5%; the authors conclude "Code review becomes the bottleneck". Press coverage cited 300M events and 700+ firms, so the paper was revised.
21. FOUND Faros telemetry (vendor, correlational). 2025 (https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering, 23 Jul 2025): 10,000+ developers, tasks +21%, PRs merged +98%, review time +91%, PR size +154%, no significant company-level DORA correlation. 2026 (https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways, 12 Apr 2026): 22,000 developers, median time in review +441.5%, PRs merged unreviewed +31.3%, incidents per PR +242.7%.
22. FOUND Amdahl inputs: Bain says coding and testing are "about 25% to 35% of the time from initial idea to product launch", with 10-15% productivity from assistants (https://www.bain.com/insights/from-pilots-to-payoff-generative-ai-in-software-development-technology-report-2025/). Atlassian (https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025): 3,500 respondents, 16% of time spent coding, 50% lose 10+ hours a week to organisational friction.
23. INFERRED (calculation): with coding at p=0.30 of lead time, a 2x coding speedup cuts lead time 15%, 5x cuts it 24%, infinite cuts it 30%. Even infinite coding speed gives only 25-35% across p=0.25-0.35. Halving needs coding above 50% of lead time or non-coding work shrinking too.

## Q3. PM time and undocumented decisions

24. NOT FOUND: a survey measuring PM time on status reporting, data chasing or internal questions. The closest proxies follow.
25. FOUND Atlassian State of Product 2026, 1,000+ professionals (https://www.mindtheproduct.com/inside-atlassians-state-of-product-2026-key-takeaways/): 49% lack time for strategy; 80% of teams do not involve engineers in ideation. diginomica (secondary, https://diginomica.com/product-teams-under-pressure-atlassian-state-product-report-role-in-flux): 49% lack time for data analysis, and AI saves 10-60 minutes a day for 50% of respondents.
26. FOUND other time proxies: Pragmatic Institute 2015 (https://www.pragmaticinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/2015AnnualSurvey.pdf) 72% tactical and 28% strategic, which is old. McKinsey 2012 (https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/hightech/our-insights/the-social-economy): 28% of the workweek on email and nearly 20% on "looking for internal information or tracking down colleagues", for interaction workers generally, not PMs.
27. FOUND McKinsey PDLC study (https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-andtelecommunications/our-insights/how-generative-ai-could-accelerate-software-product-time-to-market): 40 PMs, 40% PM productivity gain, about 5% faster time to market over a six-month PDLC. The page did not show the date. This is a small study, and it supports Amdahl: faster PM work moved total time by about 5%.
28. FOUND decision latency: Productboard 2024 (https://productboard.com/ebook/2024-product-excellence-report): 70% of large enterprises take 1-2 months for key product decisions, and 29% say initiatives seldom or never meet expected timelines. airfocus (n=169, https://airfocus.com/resources/ebooks/great-product-ops-report/): 65% of team members say feedback rarely informs decisions, and 92% say the loudest voice often wins.
29. FOUND McKinsey 2019 (https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/decision-making-in-the-age-of-urgency): n=1,259; managers spend about 37% of time on decisions, 61% say most of it is used ineffectively, only 20% say their organisation excels.
30. FOUND ADRs: Chalmers 2023 (https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/538920) is a two-month action study with no quantitative results. arXiv 2604.27333 (33 students) has no cost figures and only cites "knowledge vaporization". INFERRED: no quantified cost of undocumented decisions exists, so frame it as a pilot metric.

## Q4. LLM-as-judge

31. FOUND Husain (https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/): binary pass/fail with a critique, not 1-5 scales. About 30 examples to find failure modes, about 100 per failure mode to validate, and below 60 the CIs are "often too wide". Split 10-20% train, 40-45% dev, 40-45% test, with 30-50 per label in dev and test. Report TPR and TNR separately because raw agreement misleads (5% errors give 95% agreement for an always-pass judge). Honeycomb reached over 90% agreement in three iterations, one case only.
32. FOUND Evals FAQ (https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/): review at least 100 traces; label 100-200 examples per failure mode, at least 50 pass and 50 fail; one judge per failure type; set TPR/TNR targets by cost of misses; no fixed cutoff.
33. FOUND numeric thresholds in Husain's GitHub skill (https://github.com/hamelsmu/evals-skills/blob/main/skills/validate-evaluator/SKILL.md): target TPR and TNR above 90% on dev, minimum above 80%. Test set run once. Bias correction theta = (p_obs + TNR - 1)/(TPR + TNR - 1), invalid when TPR + TNR - 1 is near 0, with a bootstrap CI. These are agent-skill heuristics, not blog text.
34. FOUND Yan (https://eugeneyan.com/writing/llm-evaluators/): prefers binary outputs. Cohen's kappa bands 0.21-0.40 fair and 0.41-0.60 moderate. He gives "no universal numeric cutoff" and benchmarks against human-human agreement (GPT-4 kappa 0.84 vs 0.97 human-human).
35. FOUND EvalGen (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272, 18 Apr 2024): "criteria drift", meaning users need criteria to grade outputs but grading outputs helps define criteria. Rubrics must be revised against real outputs.
36. INFERRED gate for a spec-conformance judge: at least 100 domain-expert labels per failure mode, about 50/50 pass/fail, dev TPR and TNR at least 90% (floor 80%), one frozen test run, and bias-corrected rates in production.

## Q5. Software factories, spec-driven development, flags

37. FOUND StrongDM (https://factory.strongdm.ai/; https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/): team of 3, founded 14 Jul 2025. Rules: code "must not be" written or reviewed by humans. Guideline of $1,000 of tokens per engineer per day. Scenarios work like a holdout set, scored as "satisfaction". A "Digital Twin Universe" clones Okta, Jira, Slack and Google Docs, Drive and Sheets.
38. FOUND Factory (https://factory.com/articles/what-is-a-software-factory, 18 Jul 2026): loop Signals, Triage, Plan, Build, Test, Review, Secure, Ship, Monitor. It warns faster patches expose "limited review capacity". Customer-reported claims: Groq 3x on features, Empower up to 50% less PR-approval and product-to-engineering Q&A delay.
39. FOUND Warp (https://docs.warp.dev/platform/software-factory/): intake from Slack, Teams, Jira, Linear, GitHub and support threads. Loop is triage, spec, implement, review, verify, ship, monitor, with human checkpoints on spec, code and product. It never merges for you.
40. FOUND Devin (https://cognition.com/blog/devin-annual-performance-review-2025, 14 Nov 2025): PR merge rate 67% against 34% the year before. It does best with "clear, upfront requirements and verifiable outcomes" and struggles with ambiguity.
41. FOUND Spec Kit (https://github.com/github/spec-kit): constitution, specify, plan, tasks, implement, converge. Kiro (https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/): requirements.md, design.md, tasks.md. The Kiro page does not mention EARS.
42. FOUND demo videos: Cursor cloud agents deliver videos, screenshots and logs, and over 30% of Cursor's merged PRs come from autonomous agents (https://cursor.com/blog/agent-computer-use, 24 Feb 2026). Willison's shot-scraper 1.10 turns a storyboard.yml into a Playwright video, written by Codex (https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/30/shot-scraper-video/).
43. INFERRED: no public example targets customer-facing release enablement (notes, training, briefings). The videos serve reviewer proof, and the factories end at "Ship/Monitor" or PR. This is the gap the proposal fills.
44. FOUND flags (https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html, Hodgson, 9 Oct 2017): release toggles ship "latent code" and should last a week or two; permissioning toggles can last years; flags are "inventory which comes with a carrying cost"; Knight Capital lost about $460M. The LaunchDarkly page failed to fetch.
45. FOUND release trains: SAFe's ART page gives no on-demand or cadence wording (https://scaledagileframework.com/agile-release-train/); Atlassian's Bundled track and Duck Creek's flag model are the closest enterprise analogues. INFERRED: daily deploys plus per-customer flag cohorts and an announced monthly "enablement train" would match these.

## Q6. Product ops benchmarks

46. FOUND scope (State of Product Ops 2025 via https://www.productledalliance.com/what-product-ops-is-and-isnt-responsible-for-in-2025/, 3 Jul 2025): alignment 93%, process 90%, tools 81%, feedback aggregation 58%, onboarding and enablement 53%, data and analytics reporting 47%. Release management is listed without a percentage.
47. FOUND structure (https://www.productledalliance.com/state-product-operations-report-2025.md): about three-quarters centralised, almost half report to the CPO, 1 in 5 teams have no formal way to measure effectiveness.
48. FOUND metrics (https://www.productledalliance.com/top-metrics-for-product-ops-in-2022/, 4 Oct 2022): Amplitude uses lead time from first discovery call to post-launch; Twilio uses the share of medium/high-impact launches supported and on time; AppDirect tracks delivery of customer commitments (74% in 2021, 90% after Q1) and a PM time-allocation survey every six months.
49. NOT FOUND: any benchmark for "% of roadmap items with written rationale" or feedback-to-decision latency. The only figure is a vendor's own synthesis (https://getperspective.ai/blog/2026-product-feedback-benchmark-report-how-fast-top-teams-turn-signal-into-shipped/markdown): fast teams 1-2 weeks, slow teams 8-12+ weeks feedback-to-shipped, no sample, "directional".
50. INFERRED: define these as your own baselined metrics. Proxy: ProductPlan 2023 (https://www.productplan.com/ebooks/2023-state-of-product-management-annual-report) says 54% of roadmaps are output-based and 44% communicate outcomes.
